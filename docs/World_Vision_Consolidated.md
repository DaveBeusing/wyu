# Worlds Yet Unseen — Consolidated World Vision & Lore

**Status:** Consolidated design canon, 13 November 2026. Design intent only; not a claim of implementation.
**Sources:** [World Vision](World_Vision.md), [Gameplay Decisions](Gameplay_Decisions_2026-11-10.md), [Gameplay Follow-up](Gameplay_Decisions_Followup_2026-11-10.md). This document is the narrative/vision entry point; detailed gameplay values remain in their source documents.

## 1. Core promise
*Worlds Yet Unseen* is a first-person, single-player survival and discovery game on procedural, persistent, deformable voxel worlds. Its emotional pillars are solitude, wonder, freedom, gradual unease and discovery. The player is free to settle, build, explore and eventually travel into space; escaping the starting planet is not a mandatory ending.

**Opening thought:** “Ich sitze hier fest, muss überleben und herausfinden, ob ich bleiben muss oder weg kann.”

**Central mysteries:** “Wer außer mir hat überlebt?” and “Warum ist dieser scheinbar perfekte Planet nicht sicher?”

Progression is driven by practical capability and discoveries, not a forced sequence of story missions or prescribed vehicle classes. WYU owns game-specific rules and lore; Groundwork remains a reusable, lore-neutral engine.

## 2. Human mission and opening
Humanity is undertaking an early, voluntary phase of interstellar colonization, not fleeing an uninhabitable Earth. A large colony ship carries thousands of cryosleeping passengers toward a probe-identified habitable world roughly 10–15 light-years away. Travel is sublight, without FTL. The planned settlement would deploy people and equipment by landing craft while the mother ship remained in orbit.

The player is a civilian colonist with basic technical training. During approach, a severe stellar event disrupts the mission before normal colonization begins; some cryogenic units function as emergency landing capsules. The player's damaged capsule reaches the planet. Its emergency systems keep the occupant asleep for months until dwindling energy forces awakening. The game opens **inside the damaged capsule**, without a playable shipboard prologue or contrived amnesia. The capsule provides limited shelter, tools, data and a local beacon, but not a full explanation of the disaster.

The final fate and location of the mother ship are **not canonically settled**. Other capsules, wrecks and small outposts may exist; some other humans may have awakened months earlier. The player should feel alone for a substantial early period. Human traces precede any actual survivor encounters.

## 3. Planet, atmosphere and exploration
The initial world appears largely Earth-like: forests, plains, mountains, water, deserts, wetlands and cold regions transition plausibly. Visual style is high-quality, lightly stylized realism, with selective alien plants, creatures, geology and bioluminescence rather than constant fantasy spectacle. Time of day, weather, terrain, elevation and biome shape exploration.

The planetary surface is fully modifiable: mining, tunnels, caves, underground bases and permanent terrain changes are core freedoms. Resources occur in finite geological deposits, including underground deposits. Basic resources must be accessible near the start. The world map is revealed by exploration/scanning rather than fully exposed; the initial scanner reaches shallow deposits, with upgrades expanding capability. Landmarks and human traces are discovered organically, not guaranteed scripted encounters.

## 4. Narrative pacing
**Early:** peaceful isolation, basic survival, first shelter and tools, a beautiful world that feels inviting. Alien clues are subtle and ambiguous; no early direct meeting with an intelligent alien or established human community.

**Middle:** growing infrastructure, exploration, abandoned human traces, mysterious artifacts and environmental anomalies. Some signals may be detected but not deciphered. Danger and uncertainty rise without removing freedom to remain and build.

**Late:** clearer proof of intelligence, possible encounters with humans and aliens, larger discoveries and more demanding environmental events. These are tonal phases, not hard level gates or scripted hour counts.

## 5. Stellar activity and storms
The star has a recurring activity pattern: a solar storm every **30 in-game days** and an extreme superstorm every third occurrence, i.e. every **90 days**. The initial sequence is weak at day 30, medium at day 60 and extreme at day 90. Each event spans a storm day and a decay day; aurora on the two preceding nights provides warning. The opening remains relatively forgiving: the first weak storm poses a radiation concern but no electronics disruption; medium storms bring temporary radio/machine problems; extreme storms temporarily disable exposed active systems without destroying batteries or draining their stored charge.

Rock cover and underground/in-mountain bases provide meaningful protection for people and equipment. Radiation protection for constructed shelters depends on suitable materials; ordinary wood is not adequate shielding. Stronger protective technology becomes available later. The storm cycle provides longer-term pressure and mystery, but **does not force evacuation or spaceflight**.

**Lore distinction:** The stellar event during the colony ship's approach is established in planning, but the detailed causal chain, ship damage and final outcome remain to be written. Alien civilization does not cause or control the storms.

## 6. Native life and alien civilization
The world supports relatively few distinctive, biome-associated animal species. Most are harmless; some can threaten the player. Wildlife interactions, including occasional predator/prey behavior, should feel emergent rather than staged. Plants provide food, materials and rare medicinal uses, with scanning needed to identify medicinal properties.

An **active, native, technologically advanced intelligent civilization** exists on the starting planet, living predominantly underground as protection from stellar conditions. Its anatomy is visibly nonhuman. Its architecture should feel organic and alien, with discernible technological details; its technical sophistication exceeds humanity's in ways not yet fully specified.

Early evidence comprises ambiguous anomalies, abandoned structures, artifacts and undecipherable radio signals. The player should not know with certainty until relatively late that a living intelligence is present. Alien materials are not directly exploitable at the start; research is needed. The civilization has noticed the colony ship but initially does not know where the scattered capsules and survivors landed.

**Open:** alien language, society, motivations, first contact, relationship to older ruins and precise technology. Do not presume universal hostility or friendship.

## 7. Player freedom, construction and progression
Players construct shelters, bases, vehicles, probes and ships freely from modular components, with snap aids but without fixed blueprints as the primary method. **Constructed buildings have no collapse/stability simulation and floating constructions are allowed**; this later gameplay decision supersedes the older vision's suggestion of building statics. Voxel terrain deformation remains independent.

Research is discovery-gated and requires real materials and time rather than abstract points. Broad progression moves from survival/tools through bases/power, machinery, vehicles and eventually spaceflight, without mandating a particular first ship or probe.

Production and automation are conveniences, **not a factory optimization game**. Solid goods travel through opaque rectangular conduits; liquids/gases through round pipes. Networks have unlimited throughput. Player inventory is weight-limited, not volume-limited. Detailed logistics, research, survival, weather and radio rules remain governed by the gameplay decision documents.

Death is player-friendly: respawn at the capsule or designated point; constructed bases, research and stored resources persist; carried equipment remains recoverable at the death location without timed despawn. No in-world resurrection explanation is required.

## 8. Spaceflight and planetary expansion
Player-built modular spacecraft travel **seamlessly** from surface through atmosphere into space, with no loading transition. Simplified Newtonian flight retains inertia and counter-thrust, with optional assistance; gravity is simplified rather than requiring exact orbital mechanics. Docking provides magnetic-feeling alignment assistance. Launch/landing fuel costs are forgiving and re-entry heating is abstracted.

A researched faster **conventional** engine enables longer journeys through ordinary acceleration; there is no warp, hyperspace, FTL, teleportation or mandatory automatic shutdown near celestial bodies. Thrust and braking consume propulsion resources; coasting at speed does not. Piloting remains manual.

Only designated pressurized compartments must be airtight and supplied with life support, not the entire ship. Breaches cause gradual, leak-size-dependent depressurization, from repairable small leaks to rapid major losses. Asteroids can be tunneled through or fully destroyed. A navigational map supports local player markers, with future sharing not architecturally excluded.

## 9. Canon precedence and boundaries
For explicit conflicts, the later agreed gameplay decisions override earlier exploratory language:
- **Building stability:** no structural-collapse simulation for constructed buildings.
- **Storm timing:** fixed 30-day cycle with every third storm extreme; “late-game threat” describes the escalation and experience, not an absence of all early storms.
- **Logistics:** unlimited conduit throughput; automation is primarily comfort.
- **Spaceflight:** conventional sublight propulsion, seamless travel and simplified gravity.
- **Atmosphere:** only declared compartments need sealing; leaks depressurize over time.

Do not convert proposals, balancing estimates or open questions into established story facts. Keep this file as the high-level canonical narrative/vision entry point; retain detailed gameplay decision documents as the authoritative source for precise mechanics until they are separately refactored.

## 10. Still to design

These are **open design topics**, not missing implementation claims.
